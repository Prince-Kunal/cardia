## A pump made of muscle

Your [heart](term:heart) is a pump made of muscle, about the size of your fist. It sits in the middle of your chest, between the lungs, tipped a little to the left.

All day and all night it squeezes and relaxes, about 60 to 100 times a minute when you are resting. Each squeeze pushes blood out through tubes called blood vessels, to every part of the body.

The pointed bottom end of the heart is the **[apex](part:apex)**. The wide top, where the big blood vessels join it, is the **[base](part:base)**. Keep these two words in mind: the lab cuts its heart slices from the apex to the base.

::scene{camera="section" highlight="ra,la,rv,lv"}

## Four rooms

Inside, the heart is hollow. It has four spaces called **chambers**:

- two on top, the **[right atrium](part:ra)** and the **[left atrium](part:la)** (together, the [atria](term:atrium)). They collect blood coming back to the heart.
- two below, the **[right ventricle](part:rv)** and the **[left ventricle](part:lv)** (the [ventricles](term:ventricle)). They are the strong pumps that push blood out.

> In pictures of the heart, right and left are the heart's own sides, as if the person were facing you. So the heart's right side is on the left of the picture.

:::quiz{type="multiple-choice" id="m1-chambers"}
Which chambers do most of the pumping?
- [ ] The atria
- [x] The ventricles
- [ ] All four chambers equally
> The atria collect blood and give the ventricles a small top-up. The thick-walled ventricles do the real pushing.
:::

::scene{camera="section" highlight="septum"}

## Two pumps side by side

A wall of muscle called the **[septum](part:septum)** splits the heart into a right side and a left side. Blood does not pass through it.

So the heart is really two pumps working side by side:

- the **right side** sends blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen;
- the **left side** sends that oxygen-rich blood to the whole body.

You will follow that journey step by step in module 3.

::scene{camera="section" highlight="tricuspid,mitral,pulmonary-valve,aortic-valve" anim="heartbeat"}

## One-way doors

Blood must only flow forward. Four **[valves](term:valve)** make sure of it. They are flaps of tissue that open when blood pushes from behind, and snap shut when it tries to flow back.

- The **[tricuspid valve](part:tricuspid)** sits between the right atrium and the right ventricle.
- The **[mitral valve](part:mitral)** sits between the left atrium and the left ventricle.
- The **[pulmonary valve](part:pulmonary-valve)** and the **[aortic valve](part:aortic-valve)** guard the exits of the two ventricles.

The "lub-dub" of a heartbeat is the sound of the valves closing.

:::quiz{type="true-false" answer="false" id="m1-valves-tf"}
Valves let blood flow either way, depending on which side pushes harder.
> Valves are one-way doors: they open for forward flow and close against backward flow.
:::

::scene{camera="short-axis" highlight="myocardium,endocardium,epicardium" labels="myocardium,endocardium,epicardium"}

## The wall

The heart's wall has three layers:

- a thin inner lining, the **[endocardium](term:endocardium)**, which touches the blood;
- a thick middle layer of muscle, the **[myocardium](term:myocardium)**, which does the squeezing;
- a thin outer layer, the **[epicardium](term:epicardium)**.

The myocardium matters most in this course. It is the muscle that dies in a heart attack, and the tissue Cardia measures.

::scene{camera="short-axis" highlight="lv,rv"}

## Why the left side is thicker

Picture a slice cut straight across the ventricles. The **[left ventricle](part:lv)** is a thick, round ring. The **[right ventricle](part:rv)** is a thin crescent wrapped around one side of it.

The left ventricle pushes blood around the whole body. That takes about five times the pressure needed to push blood through the lungs next door, which is the right ventricle's job. Harder work builds a thicker wall.

> The lab works with guinea pig hearts. They are much smaller than a human heart, but they have the same four chambers, the same valves and the same thick left ventricle.

:::quiz{type="multiple-choice" id="m1-thick"}
Why is the wall of the left ventricle thicker than the wall of the right ventricle?
- [ ] It holds much more blood
- [x] It pumps blood to the whole body, which needs higher pressure
- [ ] It pushes blood into the lungs
> The left ventricle works against the high pressure of the body's circulation; the right ventricle only has to reach the lungs.
:::
