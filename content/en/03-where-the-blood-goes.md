## Blood on a loop

Blood travels in a loop. It leaves the heart, goes around the body, comes back, goes through the lungs, comes back to the heart, and starts again. In a resting adult, the heart pumps about as much blood each minute as the whole body holds, so a drop of blood makes the trip in roughly a minute.

On the way, blood carries oxygen from the lungs to every cell, and carries waste carbon dioxide back to the lungs to be breathed out.

> In pictures, oxygen-rich blood is drawn red and oxygen-poor blood blue. Real blood is always red: oxygen-poor blood is just a darker red.

::scene{camera="section" highlight="svc,ivc,ra" anim="flow"}

## Back from the body

Blood that has given its oxygen to the body comes back through two large [veins](term:vein), the [venae cavae](term:vena-cava): the **[superior vena cava](part:svc)** from the head and arms, and the **[inferior vena cava](part:ivc)** from the rest of the body.

Both empty into the **[right atrium](part:ra)**.

::scene{camera="section" highlight="tricuspid,rv,pulmonary-valve,pulmonary-trunk" anim="flow"}

## Off to the lungs

From the right atrium, blood passes the **[tricuspid valve](part:tricuspid)** into the **[right ventricle](part:rv)**.

The right ventricle squeezes and pushes it through the **[pulmonary valve](part:pulmonary-valve)** into the **[pulmonary artery](part:pulmonary-trunk)**, which splits in two, one branch for each lung.

In the lungs, blood flows through tiny vessels wrapped around the air sacs. It drops off carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen.

:::quiz{type="true-false" answer="false" id="m3-pa-tf"}
The pulmonary artery carries oxygen-rich blood.
> It carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs. An [artery](term:artery) is any vessel that carries blood away from the heart, whether the blood is rich in oxygen or not.
:::

::scene{camera="section" highlight="pulmonary-veins,la,mitral,lv" anim="flow"}

## Back to the heart

Oxygen-rich blood returns from the lungs through the **[pulmonary veins](part:pulmonary-veins)** into the **[left atrium](part:la)**. It passes the **[mitral valve](part:mitral)** into the **[left ventricle](part:lv)**.

:::quiz{type="fill-blank" id="m3-fill"}
Blood coming back from the lungs enters the [[left atrium]].
:::

::scene{camera="section" highlight="lv,aortic-valve,aorta" anim="flow"}

## Out to the body

The left ventricle gives the strongest squeeze of all. It pushes blood through the **[aortic valve](part:aortic-valve)** into the **[aorta](part:aorta)**, the body's largest artery.

Branches of the aorta carry blood to every organ. The very first branches, just above the aortic valve, go back to feed the heart muscle itself. They are the coronary arteries, and they are where a heart attack begins (module 5).

::scene{camera="section" anim="flow" labels="all"}

## Two circuits, one heart

So the heart drives two circuits, one after the other:

- the **[pulmonary circulation](term:pulmonary-circulation)**: right ventricle, lungs, left atrium;
- the **[systemic circulation](term:systemic-circulation)**: left ventricle, body, right atrium.

Both sides of the heart pump the same amount of blood with each beat. They have to: whatever goes into the lungs must come back out.
